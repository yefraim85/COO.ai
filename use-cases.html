Finance & operations control.

Where documents, money, approvals, recurring reviews and accountability intersect — the fastest place to create measurable value.

“Pay this supplier invoice.”

Checks entity, contract, scope, tax, withholding, duplicates, bank-detail changes, cash source and approvals — then pays and reconciles to the bank and ledger.

“Review all hourly suppliers this month.”

Compares actual hours to plans, caps and deliverables, computes accrued debt and estimate-at-completion, and raises the management actions needed.

“Onboard this new supplier.”

Collects corporate, tax, banking, insurance and compliance documents, runs risk and contract checks, and activates only when evidence and approvals are complete.

“Which contracts and documents expire soon?”

Reads renewal and notice clauses, tracks validity, and launches renewal, replacement or termination before the last safe decision point.

“Can we approve this budget request?”

Shows budget, commitments, forecast and cash impact; proposes defer, reduce, reallocate or approve; routes authority and updates downstream limits.

“Add this salary expense to the cashflow.”

De-duplicates, adds it with the right timing, recalculates runway, and shows the first expected cash-shortage date.

“Are we heading toward a cash shortage?”

Separates confirmed from uncertain flows, models base and downside, explains the lowest cash point, and recommends actions — then monitors them.

“Close the month and explain what changed.”

Coordinates reconciliations and accruals, finds missing invoices and unrecorded liabilities, explains variance, and produces the management review.

“Which entity and bank account should handle this?”

Weighs party, jurisdiction, tax, currency, licenses and authority, then propagates the choice to contracts, invoices, payments and reporting.

Expansion

The same company model, reaching every corner.

Once operations run on one model, the same engine handles people, customers, projects, governance and risk — no new system to adopt.

People & access

“We hired Maya.”

Works backward from the start date to prepare contracts, payroll, accounts, equipment and training — closing only when readiness is evidenced.

“Daniel is leaving the company.”

Finds every account, device, project and obligation he owns; coordinates handover, access revocation, asset return and final pay; produces a closure pack.

“Grant temporary production access.”

Checks role, device posture, purpose, duration and segregation of duties; provisions on approval; and verifies expiry or removal.

Customers & revenue

“The customer signed — start onboarding and billing.”

Extracts commercial terms, creates the records, launches onboarding, schedules billing milestones, and tracks acceptance and first-billing readiness.

“This customer hasn't paid.”

Confirms delivery and due date, checks disputes and promises, runs the right collection path, and updates timing in the cash forecast.

“Process this refund.”

Verifies entitlement, computes refund and tax impact, executes through the correct channel, reconciles, and tracks repeat causes.

Projects & governance

“Kick off this project.”

Creates the space, budget, milestones, dependencies, decision rights and reporting cadence, and maintains a connected timeline.

“This project is over budget.”

Separates spent, committed, accrued and forecast, explains whether it's scope, rate, volume or delay, models options, and routes the decision.

“Prepare the board meeting.”

Gathers approved data, drafts the agenda and decision materials, checks consistency, and captures decisions and tracks commitments.

Risk, knowledge & management

“A critical supplier failed.”

Maps every dependent system, team, customer and payment, activates continuity plans, evaluates alternatives, and updates sourcing risk.

“We have a security incident.”

Classifies severity, opens a restricted workspace, coordinates containment and recovery, preserves evidence, and checks notification obligations.

“What is going wrong today?”

A role-specific brief of only material changes, risks and decisions — with why each matters, linked to evidence, and followed through.

“What is the approved procedure?”

Answers from current approved policy, respects permissions, cites the source, flags stale or conflicting SOPs, and can launch the workflow.

“Why does this process keep failing?”

Clusters deviations, drafts a process improvement, tests it against history, routes approval, and monitors the new version.
